\begin{abstract}
Latent world models enable action planning but struggle with distribution shifts after deployment. Test-time adaptation addresses these shifts within an episode, yet resets afterward, forcing agents to repeatedly relearn recurring changes. We find that carrying adapted weights forward fails: consecutive updates are nearly orthogonal (mean cosine 0.018), and inheriting them cuts success from 62\% to 48\%. We therefore retain the observed transitions, not the weight updates. Inspired by complementary learning systems, we propose SE-JEPA, which pairs fast within-episode adaptation with slow cross-episode consolidation. The original pretrained weights remain fixed, while a separate slow predictor for each self-discovered domain learns from replayed trajectories. Its own prediction errors guide what to store and when to trust a learned predictor. No shift labels or task boundaries are needed, and both gradient-based and sampling-based planners benefit. SE-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art results, improving held-out success over AdaJEPA by 15.4--17.1 percentage points on unseen PushObj shapes and 15.9--18.7 points on PushT visual shifts. Relative to pretrained DINO-WM, it reduces Chamfer distance by 48.6\% on rope and 26.7\% on granular, with continued gains over deployment. The source code~will~be~made~public.
\end{abstract}
