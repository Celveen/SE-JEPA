Latent world models enable action planning but struggle with distribution shifts after deployment. Test-time adaptation addresses these shifts within an episode, yet resets afterward, forcing agents to repeatedly relearn recurring changes. We find that carrying adapted weights forward fails: consecutive updates are nearly orthogonal (mean cosine 0.018), and inheriting them cuts success from 62% to 48%. We therefore retain the observed transitions, not the weight updates. SE-JEPA, inspired by complementary learning systems, pairs fast within-episode adaptation with slow cross-episode consolidation, replaying trajectories into one slow predictor per self-discovered domain. Supervised only by its own prediction errors, it decides what to store and when to trust a learned predictor for planning. The pretrained model is never overwritten, no shift labels or task boundaries are needed, and both gradient-based and sampling-based planners benefit. SE-JEPA sets a new state of the art on unseen shapes, visual shifts, and rope and granular manipulation, improving held-out success over AdaJEPA by 15-19 points and reducing Chamfer distance by up to 49% relative to the pretrained model, and keeps improving the longer it is deployed.
